ASTRA 2026 · Domain 04 · Satellite Communication, Navigation & Connectivity

Find people when the network is gone.

ASTRA RESQ uses India's own navigation satellites (NavIC / IRNSS) to fix a person's position in places with no mobile signal, carries their SOS out through a store-and-forward link, and puts every victim on an ISRO Bhuvan map so rescue teams can be assigned and tracked.

The problem

01

No signal where disasters happen

Flash floods, landslides and cloudbursts in the Himalaya (Kedarnath 2013, Chamoli 2021) hit valleys with little or no mobile coverage - and take down what towers exist.

02

"Somewhere near the trail" isn't a location

Rescuers lose hours searching. A metre-level satellite fix shared with the SOS turns a search into a pickup.

03

Alerts arrive as noise

Calls, messages and radio reports reach control rooms unstructured. Teams need one map of who needs help, how badly, and who is closest.

What's real in this prototype

  • NMEA 0183 parsing (GGA, RMC, GSV) with checksum validation, including the NavIC $GI talker
  • Separate NavIC vs GPS satellite counts, sky plot and signal strengths
  • Offline store-and-forward queue with idempotent retry
  • Bhuvan WMS base map, nearest-team routing by great-circle distance
  • Hardware path: device/serial_bridge.py reads a real NavIC receiver over serial

What's simulated

  • The receiver: the victim device replays a generated NMEA log near Kedarnath (or uses browser location, clearly labelled as not NavIC)
  • The comms link: a network toggle stands in for LoRa mesh / satellite / SMS - the queue behaviour is real
  • Rescue teams are seed data in data/teams.json
  • No database: in-memory server store plus a browser mirror (see README)
